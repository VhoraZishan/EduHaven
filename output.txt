1. Content Model — Post Types
EduHaven supports six distinct post types. Every post must be education-related in some way — academic, cultural, political, informational, or recreational learning. The post type is chosen by the author at creation time and displayed visibly on every post card.


All post types are equal in access. No type is locked behind verification or premium. Content only requires an education-relevant angle.

2. Communication System
Communication on EduHaven is structured in two layers: the post itself (the primary content unit) and the response layer beneath it.

2.1 Post Layer
Every post has a title, a body (rich text), a type tag, subject tags, and community assignment.
Rich text supports: headings, bold/italic, bullet lists, code blocks, LaTeX math notation, embedded images, embedded video (YouTube/link), and hyperlinks.
Posts can be edited by the author. Edit history is visible to readers.
Posts can be locked by moderators (no new comments), pinned, or marked as Resolved (for Questions).

2.2 Response Layer — Comments & Answers

2.3 Interactions
Every post and every comment/answer supports the following interactions:


Upvote/Downvote: visible net score shown on post. Raw counts shown on hover.
Bookmark: saved to user's private bookmark list, accessible from their profile.
Share: generates a shareable link. Can share to clipboard or external platforms.
Report: sends to moderation queue with reason selection.
Comments additionally support: Reply (one level deep on Discussions, unlimited on Answers).

3.1 Communities (Spaces)
EduHaven is organized into Communities — subject-based spaces that users can browse and join. They work similarly to Reddit's subreddits but are purpose-built for education.

5. User Identity & Verification System
EduHaven uses a layered identity system. Every user has a role tag and optionally an institutional affiliation tag. These increase trust and signal context, but do not restrict access to content.

5.1 Role Tags
Every user selects a primary role tag displayed on their profile and posts. Multiple can be held.


Role tags are self-declared by default. They become verified through the verification process described below.

5.2 Institutional Affiliation Tags
Users can link their account to an educational institution — a university, school, college, or research body. Examples:

Student @ MIT · Class of 2026
Faculty @ University of Edinburgh
Alumnus @ IIT Bombay

Affiliation tags are displayed on the user's profile and optionally on their posts. They signal credibility without gatekeeping content.

5.3 Verification Process
Verification confirms that a user's role tag and/or institutional affiliation is genuine. It is optional but encouraged.


Verification data is handled with strict privacy. Documents are reviewed and then deleted. EduHaven stores only the verified status, not the document itself.

6. Monetization Model
Core principle: All content on EduHaven is free to access. No post, community, or feature that affects learning is ever paywalled.

6.2 EduHaven Premium — What it Unlocks
Premium is purely cosmetic and convenience-based. It does not provide any academic advantage or content access advantage.


Premium users do NOT get: boosted feed ranking, access to exclusive content, ability to bypass moderation, or any academic benefit.

7. Moderation & Safety
EduHaven's moderation philosophy is minimal intervention. Content is never removed for being controversial, offensive, or politically inconvenient. Moderation exists only to enforce the core prohibited list.

EduHaven
Product Foundation Document
Version 1.0  |  March 2026

1. Vision
EduHaven is a free, open, and student-first knowledge forum — a place where anyone can learn, share, ask, and discuss without barriers, institutional bias, or censorship. It exists to hold education and the free exchange of ideas at the highest regard.

"A haven for every learner. No gatekeepers. No bias. Just knowledge."

2. Mission
To build the most open and trustworthy educational discussion platform on the internet — one that students, self-learners, and curious minds can rely on as a completely independent, community-driven resource.
3. Core Ideals
These are the non-negotiable principles that EduHaven is built on. Every product decision must be measured against them.


4. Target Audience
Primary — Students
The core audience. EduHaven is built for students at all levels: high school, university, and beyond. They come to ask questions, discuss topics, share notes, find study guidance, and connect with peers studying similar subjects.

Secondary — Education-Adjacent Individuals
Tutors, recent graduates, self-learners, and hobbyists who are passionate about knowledge and learning. They contribute expertise, mentor students, and enrich the quality of discussion.

Tertiary — General Public
Anyone is welcome. Educators, professionals, and curious minds may participate, but the platform is not optimized specifically for them.

5. Platform Positioning

6. Community Policies
6.1 What is Encouraged
Asking questions on any academic or educational topic
Sharing study materials, notes, guides, and resources
Constructive debate and disagreement — backed by reasoning
Helping others understand difficult concepts
Expressing unpopular or minority academic viewpoints
Fun, humor, and lighter educational discussion

6.2 What is Prohibited
Harassment, targeted abuse, or personal attacks on users
Spam, low-effort trolling, or deliberate disruption
Illegal content of any kind
Plagiarism presented as original work without attribution
Doxxing or sharing private personal information

6.3 Moderation Philosophy
EduHaven practices minimal moderation. Moderators do not remove content for being controversial, offensive to some, politically inconvenient, or academically unpopular. Removal is reserved strictly for content that is abusive, illegal, or constitutes spam. This policy is absolute and applies equally regardless of the viewpoint expressed.

7. Product Goals
Short-Term Goals
Establish a stable, performant core platform
Build the foundational community experience: posts, comments, profiles
Ship content discovery features: tags, categories, search
Implement reputation and quality signals: upvotes, helpful marks
Ensure security, spam prevention, and abuse reporting are solid

Long-Term Goals
Become the go-to independent forum for student discussion globally
Support subject-specific communities (subreddit-style spaces)
Build resource libraries: notes, PDFs, study guides contributed by users
Introduce peer recognition systems: badges, expertise levels
Maintain zero institutional affiliation as scale grows

8. Feature Priority Roadmap
Features are ranked by importance to the core product experience, not by implementation order.


9. What EduHaven Is Not
Clarity on what EduHaven does not aim to be is as important as what it does.


10. Success Metrics
EduHaven measures success through the health of its community and the quality of its content, not vanity metrics.


This document defines the foundational principles of EduHaven.
All future product decisions should be measured against these ideals.
